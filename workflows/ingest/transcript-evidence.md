# Transcript Evidence

Use this reference whenever an ingest source contains transcribed speech: a
speaker-labeled call or meeting, interview, podcast, video transcript,
timestamped subtitle file, or notes attributing statements to named people.
The trigger is transcript-like evidence, not one particular `source_type`.

## Purpose

Prevent unsupported speaker attribution, timestamp drift, interaction
reversal, and false claims of independent corroboration when transcript text
becomes durable wiki knowledge.

## Verification contract

Before authoring a claim that depends on who spoke, exact wording, timing,
sequence, or interaction direction:

1. Reopen the raw transcript or faithful transcript text. A source-page summary
   is not sufficient for an exact quotation, named-speaker attribution,
   timestamp, or interaction claim.
2. Confirm the speaker delivered the relevant words. Do not infer a speaker
   from participant order, attendance, nearby dialogue, host metadata, or
   another participant's notes.
3. Use quotation marks only for verbatim wording. Render compressed, cleaned,
   or synthesized wording as a paraphrase.
4. When timestamps exist, verify the location of the supporting statement—not
   merely the beginning of the surrounding topic.
5. Preserve missing precision. If the transcript lacks a reliable speaker label
   or timestamp, retain that uncertainty or omit the attribution.
6. Preserve interaction direction. Distinguish a concern, disclosure,
   correction, confirmation, question, and inference.
7. Separate attendance from authorship. Presence in a conversation does not
   establish who spoke or authored its notes.
8. Count evidence honestly. Track source artifacts, conversations, distinct
   speakers, and repeated statements separately. Repeated appearances by one
   speaker are not independent voices.
9. Lower claim precision when diarization, transcription, or timestamping is
   unreliable.

## Authoring patterns

- Exact quotation: identify the verified speaker, verified timestamp when
  present, and source page.
- Paraphrase: state that the speaker described or reported the point rather
  than presenting cleaned wording as a quotation.
- Interaction summary: name the direction explicitly—for example, one
  participant raised a concern and another confirmed it.
- Cross-source synthesis: state both the number of artifacts and the number of
  distinct speakers when independence matters.
- Unresolved attribution: record the statement as unattributed or uncertain;
  do not guess.

## Final check

Before landing a transcript-dependent claim, confirm:

- the raw artifact was checked;
- speaker identity is supported;
- quoted wording is verbatim or rendered as paraphrase;
- any timestamp points to the supporting statement;
- interaction direction is preserved;
- independence counts distinguish artifacts, conversations, and speakers; and
- unresolved ambiguity remains visible.
